Stock Span Problem SolverA high-performance C++ implementation to solve the classic Stock Span Problem using a Monotonic Stack. This approach achieves an optimal linear time complexity, making it vastly superior to a brute-force nested loop solution.📈 The ProblemThe stock span problem calculates the span of a stock's price for all $n$ days. The span of the stock's price on a given day $i$ is defined as the maximum number of consecutive days (leading up to and including day $i$) that the stock price was less than or equal to its price on that day.For example, if the prices are [100, 80, 60, 70]:Day 0 (100): No previous days. Span = 1.Day 1 (80): 80 is less than 100. Span = 1.Day 2 (60): 60 is less than 80. Span = 1.Day 3 (70): 70 is greater than 60 but less than 80. Consecutively, it is greater than or equal to itself and day 2's price. Span = 2.⚡ Algorithm & OptimizationInstead of checking all previous days for every single element—which takes $O(n^2)$ time—this code uses a Monotonic Decreasing Stack storing the indices of the days.Computational MechanicsThe Pop Phase: For the current day $i$, we look at the index at the top of the stack. If the price at that index is less than or equal to the current day's price (price[s.top()] <= price[i]), it can no longer act as a "blocking high boundary." We pop it out. We repeat this until we find a price strictly greater than the current day's price, or the stack becomes empty.The Calculation Phase:If the stack is empty: The current day's price is the highest seen so far. Its span is simply all days up to now: i + 1.If the stack is not empty: The element remaining at s.top() is the nearest previous day with a strictly greater price. The span is the distance between them: i - s.top().The Push Phase: Push the current day's index $i$ onto the stack to act as a potential boundary for future days.
